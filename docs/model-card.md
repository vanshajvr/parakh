## What it predicts

For each of 20 large NIFTY 50 stocks, after each trading day's close: **the probability
that the stock beats the NIFTY 50 index over the next 5 trading days.** The product turns that
probability into a *Bullish lean* (likely to outperform), a *Bearish lean* (likely to lag), or *No call*.

- **Intended use:** education. It helps beginners see what price data says about a stock compared
  with simply holding an index fund, and how reliable that has been.
- **Not intended for:** trading decisions, personalised advice, intraday use, or any stock outside the list.

## Data

- Daily adjusted prices from Yahoo Finance (via `yfinance`), 2016-01-01 onwards, plus the NIFTY 50 index.
- 52,140 labelled stock-days after indicator warm-up.
- Universe: HDFCBANK, ICICIBANK, SBIN, KOTAKBANK, AXISBANK, BAJFINANCE, TCS, INFY, RELIANCE, NTPC, ITC, HINDUNILVR, ASIANPAINT, TITAN, MARUTI, LT, TATASTEEL, ULTRACEMCO, BHARTIARTL, SUNPHARMA.

## The model

**Reversal rule:** stocks that did worst over the past week, compared with the other stocks, lean towards beating the index over the next week. Nothing is learned except how strong that tendency has been (the calibration).

| Input | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ret_5d_vs_peers` | Return over the past week compared with the average of the 20 stocks |

## How it was chosen

The rules were written down before the experiments were run (decision D-022):

- **Research years:** 2019–2024. Each year is predicted by a model trained only on earlier data,
  with a 5-day gap so no training outcome overlaps the test year.
- **Holdout:** 2025 onwards. It is reported below, but it is never used to choose or tune anything.
- **Main measure:** daily rank IC, the correlation between each day's probabilities and how the stocks actually did
  against the index. The t-stat uses every 5th trading day, because neighbouring days share most of
  their 5-day price move.
- **The bar:** IC t-stat of at least 2.0, at most one negative research year, and calibration error of at most 2%.
- **Simpler wins:** the models are tried simplest first. A more complex one is chosen only if it passes the bar
  and beats the current choice's IC by at least 0.005.

## Calibration and "No call"

- **Calibration:** Platt scaling fitted on out-of-fold scores. Each year of the training window is scored by a model
  trained only on earlier years, and each day's scores are standardised before calibrating. If the scores haven't
  lined up with outcomes, the model abstains instead of flipping its ranking (decision D-023). Probabilities are
  centred on 50% within each day (decision D-011).
- **"No call" band:** sized on those out-of-fold scores so that about 25% of stock-days
  get a call. The more confident half of calls is labelled *Stronger edge*, the rest *Slight edge* (decision D-010).

## Honest summary

- In the research years (2019–2024) the model passed the bar set in advance: a rank IC of +0.029 with a t-stat of 2.39. That is a **small but real-looking edge**, not a large one. On the holdout (2025-01-01 to 2026-10-12), which played no part in choosing, it did not hold up well: rank IC +0.018 (t-stat 0.98) and 48.8% of 2,181 calls right.
- Across all years, 51.4% of calls were right, against 50% for a coin flip, with a calibration error
  of 0.7%.
- Over 5 days, stocks with a bullish lean did +0.09% relative to the index on average,
  and stocks with a bearish lean did -0.10%. The gap (+0.18%) is **smaller than a typical
  round-trip trading cost of about 0.25%**, so trading on these signals alone would not be expected to make money.
- Per-stock hit rates are shown with a likely range. With a few hundred overlapping calls per stock, most of the
  spread between stocks is within what chance alone produces.

## Limitations

- Uses price and volume only: no fundamentals, news or earnings dates.
- Large, liquid stocks are the hardest to predict. This is expected behaviour, not a bug.
- yfinance data can be delayed or revised. Yahoo rewrites adjusted price history after dividends and
  corporate actions, so the simulated numbers above can shift slightly between retrains (we have
  seen hit rates move by about 0.2 points). Live calls can't shift: each one is stored when it's published.
- Past out-of-sample performance does not guarantee future performance. The live ledger is the real test.
